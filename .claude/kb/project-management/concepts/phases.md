# PMBOK Process Groups

> **Purpose**: The five process groups a project moves through, and why they overlap instead of running in strict sequence
> **Confidence**: 0.85
> **MCP Validated**: not applicable — house distillation of PMBOK, not MCP-fed

## Overview

A project is a temporary effort producing a unique product, service, or result — it has a deadline, unlike ongoing operations. PMBOK groups the work into five process groups. They are not phases in a waterfall sense: several run concurrently, and the boundary between "planning ends" and "execution starts" is fuzzy by design.

## The five groups

| Group | Objective |
|-------|-----------|
| **Initiating** | Align stakeholder expectations with the project's objective; define initial scope; allocate resources |
| **Planning** | Define the total scope; refine objectives; decide the actions needed to reach them |
| **Execution** | Coordinate resources, manage stakeholder engagement, perform the work in the plan |
| **Monitoring & Controlling** | Track, review, and adjust progress and performance; catch where change is needed and initiate it |
| **Closing** | Formally close the project, phase, or contract; verify every group's work was completed correctly |

## Why / How / What / When

Before planning anything, answer in this order:

1. **Why** — the objective. Needs a measurable goal, because the goal is what proves success or failure later.
2. **How** and **What** — the scope. What gets built or delivered, and by what approach.
3. **When** — only after Why/How/What are answered does a schedule mean anything.

The most common failure is starting at execution with none of the three answered — teams that "just start building" and only produce a charter or plan retroactively, if ever.

## Quick Reference

| Signal | Group active |
|--------|---------------|
| Kickoff meeting being scheduled | Initiating |
| Scope/timeline still being negotiated | Planning |
| Tasks moving through the tracker | Execution |
| Weekly progress/risk reports going out | Monitoring & Controlling (runs alongside Execution) |
| Final deliverables being signed off | Closing |

## Common Mistakes

### Wrong

Treating the five groups as sequential gates — "we're done with Planning, moving to Execution now, planning is closed."

### Correct

Planning continues to refine throughout Execution as reality surfaces gaps; Monitoring & Controlling runs the entire time, not as a group visited once near the end.
